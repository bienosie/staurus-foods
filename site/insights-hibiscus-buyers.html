Buying dried hibiscus from Africa: practical specifications for importers

Dried hibiscus, also known as roselle, can be an ingredient for tea, botanical blends and beverage manufacturing. A clear specification comes before a price comparison.

Start with botanical identity and intended use

Confirm that the product is dried edible roselle calyces, commonly associated with Hibiscus sabdariffa. Specify whether the material is intended for infusion, beverage manufacture or another application. Origin, harvest period and processing method should be documented for the actual lot.

Define measurable acceptance criteria

Ask suppliers to identify moisture limits, foreign matter, colour expectations, broken material and packing requirements. Discuss food safety testing, including microbial contamination and pesticide residues, with reference to the importing country's rules and buyer standards. A photograph alone cannot establish compliance.

Match samples to a traceable lot

A representative sample should be connected to a defined batch, not merely chosen for appearance. Agree who commissions testing, how the samples are sealed and what happens if bulk goods differ from the accepted specification.

Compare the full commercial offer

Record purchase quantity, packing, Incoterm, origin point, freight responsibility, inspection, payment conditions and permitted claims. A lower price is not necessarily better if it excludes testing or causes delays at the border.
